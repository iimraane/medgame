- (direction) Use the selected daily-need consultation route: the player resolves a concrete routine need rather than inferring a disease from symptoms.
- (decision) Reuse the existing shared patient personality system for every pharmacy client; select exactly two distinct traits and layer them over the case.
- (decision) Adopt the established cumulative 10-level pool progression for pharmacy: 3, 6, 10, 15, 20, 22, 24, 26, 28, and 30 candidate products.
- (capability) CAP-1: The player can progress through ten pharmacy levels with cumulative, deterministic product pools so each level unlock is demonstrable.
- (capability) CAP-2: The player can conduct a daily-need counter consultation whose client request, context, and two shared traits create varied dialogue without diagnostic play.
- (capability) CAP-3: The player can select an appropriate product or safe alternative action from a server-authored catalog after the consultation.
- (capability) CAP-4: The player can submit a short free-text dispensing counsel in a confirmation popup covering when, how, and any relevant specific risk.
- (capability) CAP-5: The system can score the product choice from 0 to 60 and the submitted counsel from 0 to 40, returning a clear debrief and a final percentage.
- (capability) CAP-6: The system can validate catalog content, evaluation fallback behavior, API contracts, and browser flows before release.
- (constraint) The evaluation must use a constrained server-side rubric and a deterministic safe fallback; a model may not invent product equivalence, clinical guidance, or scores outside the 60 plus 40 split.
- (constraint) Future catalog entries must be authoritative, age and context aware, and reviewed before publication; the game must not diagnose, prescribe, or replace professional care.
- (constraint) Scoring must treat near alternatives through a finite, authored equivalence matrix rather than open-ended model similarity.
- (non-goal) This first Pharmacy specification does not select the thirty products, define the tools, reproduce a real prescription workflow, or provide live medical advice.
- (assumption) Because the user said to use the same model as Nursing or Psychology, this specification assumes the exact cumulative pool sizes 3, 6, 10, 15, 20, 22, 24, 26, 28, 30.
- (assumption) The dispensing popup is assumed to use a short free-text counsel entry, with prompts for timing, administration, and a relevant risk.
- (question) What score is required to pass and unlock progression: the existing 70 percent threshold, or a pharmacy-specific threshold?
- (question) May a near alternative ever receive all 60 product points, or should the exact intended product always retain a score advantage?
- (question) Should the initial thirty-product catalog be limited to non-prescription medicines, or also include health products and prescription-only scenarios?
- (question) When a routine request is unsuitable, is choosing orientation or no sale a first-class correct result?
- (note) Regulatory research supports a counter-advice model: French pharmacy guidance requires information and advice adapted to the individual situation, including posology, administration, and precautions, while a pharmacist must not diagnose.
- (note) ANSM safe-use guidance supports screening for other medicines, allergies, pregnancy or changing health situations, and respecting timing and duration.
- (event) Express specification mode selected because the input gives the gameplay loop and scoring contract but defers product catalog and tools.
- (event) Coherence validation passed: six uniquely identified capabilities each have intent and success; constraints, non-goals, assumptions, and open questions are explicit.
- (event) Preservation validation passed: daily-need route, shared two-trait clients, ten pool sizes, 60 plus 40 scoring, popup counsel, model nuance, research guardrails, deferred catalog/tools, and unresolved decisions are represented.
- (event) Specification rendered with three companions: campaign, evaluation, and research guardrails.
- (decision) Replace the two-trait-only client model: Pharmacy clients use the complete existing personality stack, including shared behavioural traits, available personality/modifier categories, case presentation style, and non-verbal cues; these alter expression but never the canonical product outcome.
- (decision) Retain the existing 70 percent mastery threshold for Pharmacy progression.
- (decision) Each product receives an authored 0-to-60 proximity score against every other catalogue product; exact product is 60 and near alternatives receive a product-specific partial score based on the ordered pair.
- (decision) The Pharmacy catalogue may include all pharmacy-dispensed product types, from non-prescription products to prescription-only medicines.
- (decision) Orientation or no-sale is not a winning selectable result in Pharmacy; generated missions must therefore be pre-vetted so a valid catalogued product exists for every playable case.
- (constraint) Prescription-only cases must model lawful dispensing context such as a valid fictional prescription or authorised renewal context; the game never asks the player to select a prescription-only medicine without that context.
- (constraint) Because refusal/orientation is excluded from scoring, unsafe or unsuitable contexts must be excluded from the playable case generator rather than turned into a forced product-selection puzzle.
- (note) Research confirms prescription-only medicines require an ordonnance and that dispensing requires analysis of prescription, administration rhythms, contraindications, interactions, and redundancies; this motivates distinct prescription-context cases and tools.
